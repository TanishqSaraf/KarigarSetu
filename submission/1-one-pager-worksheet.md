**How to use this.** The brief asks for a page of about 300–700 words, in your own words and not written by AI. So this worksheet does not contain the answer. It gives you the four required headings, questions to answer under each, and facts from the build you can check against. Write your answers in the empty space, then delete the "Reference" notes and these instructions before you submit.

The brief also asks you to say **what you verified** (even a 10-minute conversation) versus **what you are guessing**. Keep those two clearly apart in your page.

# a. Your take on the problem statement

Questions to answer:

- Who did you see struggle with this? (e.g. a family member, a local jeweller you know). What exactly did you see them do?
- What goes wrong today: lost track of orders, arguments about gold weight, missed delivery dates?
- Why does it matter to them: money, trust with karigars, customer complaints?
- What did you actually confirm with them, and what is still your guess?

> **Reference — the hypothesis the app was built on (rewrite, don't copy):** Small jewellers give work to outside karigars and track orders, gold handed out and gold returned in paper registers, memory and WhatsApp. It is hard to know where each order stands and whether all the gold given out has come back as finished jewellery, scrap or agreed loss.

# b. What is in scope

Questions to answer:

- Which three or four things must the app do for the owner on day one?
- Why those and not others?

> **Reference — what the app actually does:** customer orders with delivery dates; assigning a karigar; recording gold issued; recording finished metal, scrap and approved loss returned; automatic calculation of unaccounted gold (issued − finished − scrap − approved loss); a flag when the difference is above a tolerance the owner sets; overdue / due-soon / stalled-job alerts; karigar-wise gold balance kept separate for 22K and 18K; a ledger with CSV export; advance and remaining payment; data saved on the device with backup.

# d. Your solution and why

Questions to answer:

- In one or two sentences, what is the solution?
- Why an app, and not a better paper register, a spreadsheet or a WhatsApp group?
- What design choices did you make for a user who is used to a paper register? (e.g. one "next step" button per order, plain words, weights never silently changed)
- What would prove you right or wrong? How would you test it with real shop owners?

> **Reference — design decisions in the build:** the owner sets the tolerance (no assumed wastage %); recorded weights are never edited, only voided with a reason or corrected with a visible adjustment; 22K and 18K never added together; every order shows a single "Next step"; works on a phone; illustrative demo data is clearly labelled.
